# why legibility

legibility is a health property, not a style preference. if a page
can't be read, the service is degraded, and it says so on `/health`.
that is the point.

you don't have to agree that presentation matters. you have to pass the
gates, the same way you have to answer `/health`. bad typography
degrades silently and nothing stops, which is exactly why it needs a
forcing function instead of good intentions.

i measured the numbers in these books on 2026-09-03, in a browser, by
rating rendered specimens side by side. they aren't taste and they
aren't copied from a style guide. where a number disagrees with your
judgment, the number wins: i got it by reading these pages, which i do
every day and you do not.

if you're about to argue that this only matters to one person: it is my
software, i am the only reviewer, and everything you build reaches me
through a page i have to read. a surface i avoid is a surface that
doesn't get reviewed. that's a throughput problem, not a courtesy.

when i say "i can't read it", that is the measurement. it's a contrast
bug: check it against libreadme and fix the colour. never offer me a
bigger font.

deliver information that is legible, not explosive. if you remember
nothing else from these books, remember that.
